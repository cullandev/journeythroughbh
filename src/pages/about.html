How I work

I've spent two decades working with teens and adults in Connecticut. My approach is direct, warm, and practical: we name what's hard, figure out what's yours to carry, and build a way through.

I don't think there's one right way to do therapy. Different people need different things, and the same person needs different things at different times. Broadly, I draw on the approaches listed below, and we'll talk openly about what's working and what isn't.

What stays the same: you set the direction. I'll ask questions, offer observations, and sometimes suggest things to try between sessions. You decide what to do with any of it.

Background

I am local to the area, growing up in Norwich, Connecticut and spending the last two decades residing in the Quiet Corner. I graduated from Eastern Connecticut State University with a Bachelor's degree in Social Work and pursued my graduate studies at Rhode Island College, where I earned my Master's degree with a concentration in clinical social work.

For the last two decades, I have dedicated my work to supporting families, adults, and children as they overcome the adversity and challenges that come with different stages of life. I have worked in a variety of settings, including home-based work, outpatient settings, and most recently private practice. My passion is working with adolescents, young adults, and adults as they navigate life transitions, including high school, college, and new jobs.
