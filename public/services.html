Specialties

Anxiety

What it is: persistent worry, panic, physical tension, or avoidance that gets in the way of work, relationships, or sleep.

Who it helps: people who are tired of managing it alone and want practical ways to lower the volume.

Depression and low mood

What it is: a stretch of feeling flat, heavy, or disconnected that doesn't lift on its own.

Who it helps: people who notice they've stopped doing things they used to care about, or who feel stuck and self-critical.

Life transitions

What it is: the disorientation that comes with big changes: a new role, a loss, a move, a relationship ending or beginning.

Who it helps: people who are functioning but feel unsteady, and want a place to sort through it.

Stress and burnout

What it is: running on empty, irritability, trouble switching off, and a sense that rest doesn't help.

Who it helps: people in demanding jobs or caregiving roles who want to recover and set limits that hold.

Grief

What it is: the response to losing someone or something that mattered. It doesn't follow a schedule.

Who it helps: people who want company in it, or who are worried their grief looks different from what they expected.

Relationships

What it is: patterns with partners, family, or friends that keep repeating, or difficulty with boundaries and communication.

Approaches

These are the methods I draw on. In practice they blend; you won't be asked to pick one.

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)
Looks at the links between thoughts, feelings, and actions, and works on changing the ones that keep you stuck. Structured and skills-focused.
Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)
Focuses less on getting rid of hard feelings and more on making room for them while doing what matters to you.
Person-centered therapy
The foundation under everything else: you are the expert on your own life, and my job is to listen closely and help you hear yourself more clearly.
